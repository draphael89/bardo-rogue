# Independent visual critique

One fresh reviewer received only the comparison images and five animation strips. No source or build history. Reversing the same pair is an order check, not another independent trial.

## Verdict

- comparison-ab: right.
- comparison-ba: left. Same exhibit wins after reversal.
- combat-comparison: right.

The bullet-boss exhibit has stronger silhouette, color separation and focal hierarchy. The orange boss and green beam explain where to look. Walls, furnishings and lighting establish spatial depth.

The right combat exhibit makes action legible through a bright curved slash, directional poses and concentrated effects. Environmental detail sits beneath the action in value.

The weaker exhibit has a coherent restrained palette, tidy borders and readable room geometry. Its oversized title, high-frequency floor tiles and large central emblem command more attention than tiny, muted actors. It reads as a prototype in these captures.

**Single biggest gap: actor-to-background hierarchy.** Combatants and actions need distinctive silhouettes and local contrast. The environment must yield attention around them. More decoration would not solve this.

## Strips

- Chain: sword poses and white arcs communicate attacking. Contacts become white shapes over overlapping sprites; individual strikes and targets become hard to distinguish.
- Dodge: compact rotating silhouette differs from standing. Movement across the emblem is visible. Faint trail contributes little at this scale.
- Brute: raised weapon and red coloration communicate anticipation. Overlap weakens weapon/contact readability. Red screen flash signals an event more clearly than its cause.
- Charger: red preparation, backward displacement and travel form the clearest directional sequence. A directional warning or forward-facing pose would improve the preparation.
- Kill: flash, localized burst, disappearance and persistent blood communicate the conclusion. Broad flash briefly washes out the scene.

## Limits

Idle room versus boss encounter, then overhead pixel art versus isometric combat, are not matched conditions. Subject scale and selected moment favor the reference exhibits. The verdict applies to these presentations, not to an equal-condition gameplay trial. Strips show pose progression, not timing quality. The reviewer did not play or hear audio.
